What Are the Weapons I Need to Bury?

A reflection on conversion, covenants, repentance, and what it means to leave our old weapons buried.

“And this they did, it being in their view a testimony to God, and also to men, that they never would use weapons again for the shedding of man’s blood.” Alma 24:18

Recently, I wrote about the father of King Lamoni and the question that touched my heart as I read his story:

What am I willing to leave behind to follow God?

After Aaron taught him about God, Jesus Christ, the Fall, the Atonement, and the plan of redemption, the king desired more than simply to believe what Aaron had taught him. He wanted to know God.

“What shall I do that I may be born of God, having this wicked spirit rooted out of my breast, and receive his Spirit, that I may be filled with joy?” Alma 22:15

He was even willing to forsake his kingdom to receive that joy.

But eventually his prayer became even more powerful:

“I will give away all my sins to know thee.” Alma 22:18

I have thought a lot about those words.

But as I continued reading and reached Alma 24, I realized that only a short time later, we are shown what “I will give away all my sins to know thee” actually looks like when it becomes a way of life.

When Conversion Becomes a Covenant

The people who had accepted the gospel—the Anti-Nephi-Lehies—had experienced a remarkable transformation.

These were people who had once participated in violence and bloodshed. But after coming to know Christ and receiving forgiveness for their sins, they made a covenant with God that they would never again shed the blood of their brethren.

Then came the test.

The Amalekites and Amulonites stirred up the unconverted Lamanites against them, and an army came to destroy them.

The Anti-Nephi-Lehies could have defended themselves.

In fact, from a human perspective, they had every reason to do so.

They were not attacking anyone.

They were not seeking war.

Their lives and families were in danger.

And yet, they refused to take up their weapons.

At first, that was difficult for me to fully understand.

Would defending themselves really have been the same as the murders they had committed before their conversion?

Perhaps not.

But I think there was something deeper happening inside them.

They had experienced the miracle of forgiveness, and they treasured that gift so deeply that they did not want to do anything that might lead them back toward the people they had once been.

They had made a covenant with God.

And that covenant was worth more to them than their own lives.

Because He Loveth Our Souls

Before we read about them burying their weapons, however, there is a beautiful detail that I think helps us understand why they were willing to do it.

Their king speaks about everything God had done for them and says:

“…because he has made these things known unto us beforehand, because he loveth our souls as well as he loveth our children; therefore, in his mercy he doth visit us by his angels, that the plan of salvation might be made known unto us as well as unto future generations.” Alma 24:14

I love those words:

Because he loveth our souls.

That changes the way I see the entire story.

They weren't simply afraid of God.

They weren't desperately trying to earn His forgiveness.

They had already experienced His mercy.

God had reached for them when they were lost.

He had sent messengers to teach them.

He had shown them the plan of salvation.

He had taught them about Christ.

He had offered them repentance.

And He had forgiven them.

Why?

Because He loved their souls.

And not only theirs.

He loved their children.

He loved the generations that would come after them.

Suddenly, burying their weapons wasn't simply about following a rule.

It was their response to mercy.

It was gratitude.

They knew what they had been before Christ, and they knew what He had made possible for them.

They had been given another chance, and they did not want to take that gift for granted.

Swords Made Bright Again

The king describes their swords as having been made bright through the blood of the Son of God (see Alma 24:15).

I love that imagery.

Their swords had once been stained with the blood of others. Their past was real. They could not undo what they had done.

But Christ had forgiven them.

Their sins had been washed away.

Their swords were, symbolically, bright again.

And they were so grateful for that cleansing that they would rather die than stain them again.

That makes me think about my own repentance.

How much do I treasure being forgiven?

Sometimes I think we become so accustomed to talking about repentance that we forget what an extraordinary miracle it actually is.

Through Jesus Christ, something that was stained can become clean again.

Something broken can be restored.

Someone who has wandered far away can come home.

The past doesn't have to determine the future.

And when we truly understand that, perhaps our question changes.

Instead of asking:

“How close can I get to sin without actually sinning?”

we begin asking:

“What can I remove from my life so that I never have to go back there again?”

They Buried Their Weapons

Then comes one of the most powerful images in the Book of Mormon.

They took their swords and other weapons and buried them deep in the earth.

They didn't simply promise not to use them.

They didn't put them somewhere nearby.

They didn't keep them around in case they changed their minds.

They buried them.

And suddenly I realized:

Those weapons represented more than swords.

They represented their old lives.

Their violence.

Their hatred.

Their anger.

Their rebellion.

The people they had been before Christ changed them.

And when they buried those weapons, perhaps they were also saying:

I am not going back.

That made the story very personal for me.

Because I may not have swords to bury.

But I certainly have weapons.

What Are My Weapons of War?

What are the things in my life that repeatedly lead me away from Christ?

Maybe my weapon is pride.

Maybe it is anger.

Maybe it is resentment that I refuse to release.

Maybe it is contention.

Maybe it is something I watch, read, or consume that slowly weakens my sensitivity to the Spirit.

Maybe it is a habit I continually excuse.

Maybe it is a relationship, an environment, or an influence that constantly pulls me toward the person I am trying to leave behind.

Maybe it is simply a distraction that has taken far more space in my heart than God has.

Whatever it is, Alma 24 makes me ask:

Am I trying to bury my weapons—or am I simply putting them away where I can easily reach them again?

There is a difference.

Sometimes we want repentance without removal.

We want to change while keeping everything that repeatedly leads us back to the same place.

But there may be moments when true repentance requires us to dig a hole.

To remove something.

To walk away.

To establish a boundary.

To say:

“Because of what Christ has done for me, I don't want this in my life anymore.”

Not because God stopped loving me when I struggled.

Not because His mercy disappeared.

But precisely because He loves my soul, and I am beginning to understand how precious that soul is to Him.

Would I Rather Die Than Break My Covenant?

There is another question in this chapter that is uncomfortable for me.

These people were literally willing to die rather than break the covenant they had made with God.

When their enemies came upon them, they went out to meet them without weapons. They bowed themselves to the earth and called upon the name of the Lord even as they were being killed.

That is a level of conversion that makes me examine my own.

Would I die rather than deny Christ?

I hope so.

But perhaps there is an even more immediate question:

Am I willing to inconvenience myself rather than break my covenants?

Am I willing to lose an opportunity?

A friendship?

Approval?

Comfort?

Popularity?

A habit I enjoy?

Something I really want?

It is easy for me to imagine giving my life for Christ in one heroic moment.

Sometimes it is much harder to give Him my will every ordinary day.

And perhaps that is where most of our weapons are actually buried.

Not in one dramatic sacrifice, but through hundreds of quiet decisions in which we say:

“Lord, I choose Thee again.”

Conversion Must Be Protected

The Anti-Nephi-Lehies teach me that conversion is precious, but it must also be protected.

They had experienced Christ.

They had received forgiveness.

They had made covenants.

But they still deliberately removed from their lives something that could take them backward.

That makes me wonder:

How seriously am I protecting my own conversion?

What am I doing to make it stronger?

Am I praying?

Am I studying the scriptures?

Am I remembering my covenants?

Am I repenting quickly?

Am I surrounding myself with voices that lead me toward Christ?

Am I feeding my testimony—or merely assuming it will always remain strong?

Conversion is not something I want to experience once and then place on a shelf.

I want to become more converted.

More firmly rooted in Christ.

More willing to surrender my heart to Him.

Until the things that once had power over me no longer do.

He Is Always Faithful

There is something else I never want to forget.

God does not abandon His covenants with us.

His love does not disappear when we struggle.

His light does not stop shining because we wander.

He remains faithful.

He continues calling.

He continues forgiving.

He continues inviting us home.

The question Alma 24 leaves in my heart is not whether He will be faithful.

It is:

Will I?

Will I treasure what Christ has done for me enough to protect my relationship with Him?

Will I value the remission of my sins enough to leave certain things buried?

Will I remember that He loves my soul—and therefore stop treating casually the things that distance that soul from Him?

What Will I Bury?

In Alma 22, the king said:

“I will give away all my sins to know thee.”

In Alma 24, his people showed us what that can look like.

They buried their weapons.

And perhaps that is the invitation for me now.

The question is no longer only:

What am I willing to leave behind to follow God?

Perhaps the next question is:

What am I willing to bury so that I never return to the person Christ is helping me leave behind?

What weapon has remained in my hands too long?

What should I stop justifying?

What should I finally place deep in the earth and leave there?

Not because I am terrified that God will stop loving me.

But because I finally understand that He loves my soul.

Because He has shown me mercy.

Because He has given me Christ.

Because He has forgiven me again and again.

Because He has given me covenants, His Spirit, His light, and the hope of eternal life.

And because none of the weapons I could possibly hold onto are worth more than that.

I want a conversion like the Anti-Nephi-Lehies—a conversion deep enough that the old weapons stay buried.

A conversion strong enough that when temptation comes calling, there is nothing left for me to pick up.

A conversion that remembers what Christ has done for me.

A conversion that can look at everything I once valued, everything that once controlled me, everything that once separated me from God, and say:

Christ is worth more.

A prayer to carry with me

Father, show me the weapons I still need to bury. Give me the courage to bury them deeply, the faith to leave them there, and the gratitude to remember why I buried them in the first place. Help me become so converted to Jesus Christ that nothing I leave behind will ever seem more valuable than the light I have found in Him.
